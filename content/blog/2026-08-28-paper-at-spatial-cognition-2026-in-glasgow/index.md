Jakub presented the paper **"How Floor Rotation Fragments 3D Cognitive Maps"** at [Spatial Cognition 2026](https://www.gla.ac.uk/research/az/spatialreasoning/sc26/), hosted by the Turner Kirk Centre for Spatial Reasoning at the University of Glasgow, 25–28 August 2026.

The credit goes to **Amirtha Varshini Raja**, the paper's first author, whose MSc thesis at the Institute for Geoinformatics this work is based on — great work, Amirtha! The study is part of the [3D Sketch Maps](/projects/3d-sketch-maps/) project, and was conducted together with Tianyi Xiao and Martin Raubal (ETH Zürich) and Angela Schwering.

Using VR navigation followed by 3D sketch mapping, the study found that rotating the floors of a multilevel building leaves within-floor memory almost untouched but cuts the odds of correctly recalling across-floor relations to roughly one-sixth — people fall back on separate floor-wise representations instead of a unified 3D cognitive map.
