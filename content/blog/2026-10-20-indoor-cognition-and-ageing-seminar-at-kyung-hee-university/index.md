As part of the [German-Korean collaboration](/blog/2026-02-13-funding-received-for-a-new-german-korean-collaboration/) between SPARC and the [Human-Centered Interior Environment Lab](https://hielab.weebly.com/faculty.html), the College of Human Ecology at [Kyung Hee University](https://www.khu.ac.kr/) in Seoul hosted a seminar on spatial cognition in indoor environments and ageing. The event was sponsored by the National Research Foundation of Korea.

Jakub presented results of the [3D Sketch Maps](/projects/3d-sketch-maps/) project in a talk titled **"What Gets Lost in Translation? Comparing How People Externalize 3D Spatial Knowledge with Different Tools"**, showing how much of people's spatial knowledge of multilevel buildings is lost when they are asked to draw it with 2D tools, and what VR-based 3D sketching recovers.

The programme brought together speakers working on the topic from several directions:

  * **Victor Schinazi** (Bond University, Australia): "Spatial Performance Assessment for Cognitive Evaluation (SPACE)"
  * **Ji Young Cho** (Kyung Hee University): "Subconstructs of Spatial Navigation Performance and Training"
  * **Suyeon Bae** (Kyung Hee University): "Designing Supportive Healthcare Environments for Older Adults: Insights from Hospital Wayfinding"
  * **Seo Ryeung Ju** (Kyung Hee University): "Towards Homelike Care Environments: Unit Care Design in South Korean Nursing Homes"

The talks sparked a fascinating discussion about what makes indoor wayfinding a specific problem for the ageing population. Hospitals and nursing homes are among the most complex buildings most people ever have to navigate, and their users are often the ones for whom navigation is hardest. The challenge sits at the intersection of how spatial abilities change with age, how such abilities can be measured, and how buildings themselves can be designed to compensate. It is exactly this intersection that our joint project with Kyung Hee University aims to address.
