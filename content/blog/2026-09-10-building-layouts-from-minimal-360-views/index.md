Our paper **"Understanding Building Layouts from Minimal 360° Views: Effects of Viewpoint Overlap, Sequencing, and Landmarks"** by Ahmed Aly and Jakub Krukar is out in the proceedings of [COSIT 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.COSIT.2026.9) (*LIPIcs*, open access).

The Art Gallery Problem gives the fewest viewpoints needed to *see* every part of a building, a natural basis for placing 360° panoramas in a virtual tour. We asked whether those hypothetical guards would actually *understand* their gallery's layout.

Fifty participants explored five buildings through minimal panoramic views and then picked the correct floor plan from similar alternatives. Visual overlap between consecutive views was not enough on its own. Landmarks visible across the overlapping views significantly improved layout recognition. Ordering the views along a route did not. Geometric coverage is not cognitive coverage.

The study is part of the [What does it take to learn a building?](/projects/learn-a-building/) project, funded by JICE seed funding. The web-based experimental pipeline is open source on [GitHub](https://github.com/ahmedsaly/LayoutRecStudy) and the raw data are on [OSF](https://osf.io/8e7pf/).
