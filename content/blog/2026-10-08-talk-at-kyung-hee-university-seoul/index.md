During his DFG-sponsored visit at [Kyung Hee University](https://www.khu.ac.kr/) in Seoul, Jakub gave a talk titled **"What do we need for human-AI alignment in architecture?"**. Here is the abstract:

> Until recently, user-centred architectural design had a simple structure: the architect anticipated how users would behave in a building and designed on the basis of those assumptions, whether explicit or implicit. Today, AI is present at every step of that process: the architect is AI-assisted; the building is encountered earlier as a complete digital model, with design responding to what the model can represent; and users' behaviour is simulated by a wider family of models not always designed for this.
>
> Two problems follow. First, automation is advancing unevenly across the design process. Generative tools quickly propose layouts, accelerating decision-making, while our ability to acquire ecologically valid evidence about how people experience those layouts has barely improved. Second, misalignment between human values and AI suggestions is hard to see. A better fit to data does not guarantee a better understanding of reality; and in the "wicked problem" of design, there is no ground truth against which a model can be scored.
>
> In the talk, I outline an agenda for architectural AI grounded in embodiment, spatial information theory, and interpretability: computational systems able to represent the aspects of space that matter to the people who inhabit it with their bodies.
